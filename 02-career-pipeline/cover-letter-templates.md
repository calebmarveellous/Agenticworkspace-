# Cover Letter Templates — Role-Specific, Pain-Point-Bridged

**STANDING RULE (locked, always applies):** lead with the value I'd help *them* achieve — a specific, concrete list tied to their stated pain point — never with a name-drop of past clients as the opening hook. Named past results (Zonia Hub, Suncious Foods, Luscents, etc.) are supporting proof, placed after the value list, not the lead.

Also non-negotiable: every letter must identify the employer's *specific, stated* pain point from the JD and address it directly. If you can't identify their pain point from the JD/company research, don't send the application yet — go find it first (recent funding news, product launch, Glassdoor complaints about growth stalling, competitor moves).

---

## TEMPLATE 1 — Growth Marketing Manager role at a funded DTC/e-commerce startup

```
Dear [Hiring Manager Name],

[Company]'s [specific detail — recent funding round / product launch / stated growth goal from JD or press] is exactly the kind of problem I'd want to own. Here's what I'd bring to it:

1. Full-funnel acquisition discipline — audience segmentation, a structured creative-testing cadence, and CAC:LTV tracking in GA4/Looker Studio, so growth doesn't outrun unit economics.
2. Direct-response offer construction (Hormozi/Suby frameworks) — meaning I don't just drive traffic, I build the value ladder that converts it.
3. A documented 90-day plan for [Company]'s [specific stated goal from JD, e.g., "Q1 international expansion" or "retention-driven growth targets"], not a generic onboarding ramp.

I've applied this exact approach before — at [Client Name], I [specific action] using [Meta Ads Manager / GA4 / etc.], moving [metric] from [X] to [Y] in [timeframe].

I'd welcome the chance to walk through exactly how I'd approach [Company]'s first 90 days.

Best,
Caleb Marvel
```

## Before sending ANY letter — 4-point check
1. Does the opening lead with what I'd help *them* achieve (a concrete list), not a client name?
2. Does it name a real employer pain point from THEIR JD/news, not a generic industry pain point?
3. Does it back the value claims with a specific named client result (Zonia Hub, Suncious Foods, etc.) placed as supporting proof, not the hook?
4. Is the tone direct and confident, zero corporate filler ("I am writing to express my interest," "I believe I would be a great fit") — cut every sentence that doesn't do work?
