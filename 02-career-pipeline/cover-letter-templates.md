# Cover Letter Templates — Role-Specific, Pain-Point-Bridged

Rule from your brief, non-negotiable: every letter must uniquely bridge a *named* past result (Zonia Hub, Suncious Foods, Luscents, etc.) to the employer's *specific, stated* pain point from the JD. If you can't identify their pain point from the JD/company research, don't send the application yet — go find it first (recent funding news, product launch, Glassdoor complaints about growth stalling, competitor moves).

---

## TEMPLATE 1 — Growth Marketing Manager role at a funded DTC/e-commerce startup

```
Dear [Hiring Manager Name],

[Company]'s [specific detail — recent funding round / product launch / stated growth goal from JD or press] caught my attention because it's the exact problem I solved for [Luscents / McDobi GLOBAL / Hopmichar]: scaling paid acquisition without letting CAC outrun LTV.

At [Client Name], I [specific action] using [Meta Ads Manager / GA4 / etc.], which moved [metric] from [X] to [Y] in [timeframe]. I'd apply the same full-funnel discipline — audience segmentation, creative testing cadence, and CAC:LTV tracking in GA4/Looker Studio — to [Company]'s [specific stated goal from JD, e.g., "Q1 international expansion" or "retention-driven growth targets"].

I also bring direct-response and offer-construction expertise (Hormozi/Suby frameworks) that most growth marketers skip — meaning I don't just drive traffic, I build the value ladder that converts it.

I'd welcome the chance to walk through exactly how I'd approach [Company]'s first 90 days.

Best,
Caleb Marvel
```

## Before sending ANY letter — 3-point check
1. Does it name a real employer pain point from THEIR JD/news, not a generic industry pain point?
2. Does it bridge to a specific named client (Zonia Hub, Suncious Foods, etc.) with a real or clearly-marked-placeholder number?
3. Is the tone direct and confident, zero corporate filler ("I am writing to express my interest," "I believe I would be a great fit") — cut every sentence that doesn't do work?
